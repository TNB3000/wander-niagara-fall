Fall is when this region cooks. The harvest comes in, the festivals stack up, and the wineries open their doors wide. Here are eight ways to taste your way through it.

## 1. Do the Niagara Grape & Wine Festival

The region's signature party. Montebello Park fills with music, food and local wine. Go for an afternoon and stay for the evening show.

## 2. Eat apple fritters at Pioneer Day

The Lincoln Museum's Pioneer Day means fresh apple fritters and food trucks. Come hungry.

## 3. Sip your way through Sip Niagara

A festival built around exactly what it sounds like. Plan a designated driver.

## 4. Spend Thanksgiving at Ball's Falls

The Ball's Falls Thanksgiving Festival is the classic fall weekend here — vendors, food and full colour in the conservation area.

## 5. Take a winery-hosted wander

The Fall Wine Trail runs through 180 Estate Winery, Sue-Ann Staff Estate Winery, Cave Spring Vineyards, Malivoire Wine Co. and Fielding Estate Winery. Then pick your event: the Grilled Cheese Extravaganza at Sue-Ann Staff, Passport to Pinot at Cave Spring, or From Beamsville to Burgundy at Le Clos.

![The barrel cellar at 180 Estate Winery](/assets/img/photos/inline-180-cellar.jpg)

## 6. Get to know your favourite restaurant's siblings

The region's best kitchens have little siblings worth hunting down: Pearl Morissette's RPM Bakehouse, Fat Rabbit's Bar Les Incompétents, Odd Bird's Odd Bar, Wellington Court's The Coach House, and Valley Restaurant's Café Pamela in Port Weller.

![Dinner at Bar Les Incompétents](/assets/img/photos/inline-les-incompetents.jpg)

## 7. Spend a morning marketing

Farmers' markets and the CultivATE Trail put the harvest straight in your hands. Baskets of plums, macs by the bushel, and squash in every colour.

![Market apples by the basket](/assets/img/photos/inline-market-apples.jpg)

## 8. Try Niagara's tastiest new collab

The Cargo Kitchen at Upper Canada Cheese Co. — a new pairing worth the drive on its own.

Pick two or three, map them into a day, and the season does the rest.
